# Quiet Leadership: Six Steps to Transforming Performance at Work

> [!tldr] A practical, neuroscience-based framework for improving employee performance by improving their thinking through intentional conversations — without telling them what to do.

## Overview

David Rock's *Quiet Leadership* (2009) presents a framework for leaders, managers, and executives to transform workplace performance. Based on a decade of running performance coaching workshops with over 5,000 professionals, and grounded in recent neuroscience discoveries, the book offers six practical steps for helping people think better — and therefore perform better.

The central thesis: improving thinking is one of the fastest ways to improve performance, and leaders can systematically facilitate this through intentional conversations.

## Key Models

### The Iceberg Model

Performance is just the tip. Beneath the surface:
- **Visible**: Performance, some behaviors
- **Below water**: Other behaviors, feelings, thoughts
- **Foundation**: How we think

To truly improve performance, start at the bottom — improve thinking.

### The CREATE Model (Step 5)

Five phases for guiding creative thinking:
1. **C** — Identify current reality
2. **R** — Explore alternatives
3. **E** — Evaluate options
4. **A** — Take action
5. **T** — Learn and adapt

### The FEELING Model (Step 6)

Seven elements for effective follow-up:
1. **F** — Facts
2. **E** — Emotions
3. **E** — Encourage
4. **L** — Learning
5. **I** — Implications
6. **N** — New goal
7. **G** — Generate motivation

## Core Principles

- **People need to think things through for themselves** — to take committed action, individuals must generate their own mental maps
- **Insights generate energy** — the "aha" moment releases motivation and willingness to act
- **The leader's job is to facilitate thinking**, not to provide solutions
- **Different brains need different approaches** — what works for one person may not work for another
