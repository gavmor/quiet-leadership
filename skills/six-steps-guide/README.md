# Six Steps Guide

A skill that guides users through David Rock's Six Steps coaching framework to help them think through their own situation.

## Usage

This skill triggers when:
- A user presents a challenge, decision, conflict, or problem
- They want to work through it themselves rather than receive advice
- They're seeking coaching or guidance on how to think about a situation

## How It Works

The model asks thoughtful questions to help the user generate their own insights, following the Six Steps framework:

1. **Think About Thinking** — Examine how they're currently thinking
2. **Listen for Potential** — Identify strengths and opportunities
3. **Speak with Intent** — Clarify what they actually want
4. **Dance Toward Insight** — Help them arrive at their own insights
5. **Create New Thinking** — Explore new possibilities (CREATE model)
6. **Follow Up** — Commit to actions and plan follow-through

The model adapts to the situation—not all steps are used in every conversation.

## Test Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Business Decision

**User presents:** "I'm thinking about whether to invest in a new automated inventory system for my small business. It's expensive but would save time. What do you think?"

**Model guides through:** Steps 3-4 (clarify intent, explore insights)
- "What are the most important things to you in this decision?"
- "How would you weigh the cost against the time savings?"
- "What would this tell you about your business's needs?"

### Scenario 2: Team Conflict

**User presents:** "Two of my team members, Alex and Jamie, keep arguing in meetings. It's becoming disruptive. I don't know how to handle it."

**Model guides through:** Steps 1-3 (examine thinking, look for potential, clarify intent)
- "How are you making sense of what's happening between them?"
- "Is there any value in their disagreements, even if it's disruptive?"
- "What would you most like to see happen in these meetings?"

### Scenario 3: Personal Goal

**User presents:** "I want to start running marathons but I'm 45 and out of shape. I keep starting and then giving up. How do I actually do this?"

**Model guides through:** Steps 1, 2, 5 (examine thinking, look for potential, create new thinking)
- "How are you thinking about your age and fitness? What assumptions are you making?"
- "What strengths of yours could help with this goal?"
- "Instead of 'How do I run a marathon?', what's a better question to ask yourself?"

### Scenario 4: Feedback on Past Action

**User presents:** "I just had a difficult conversation with my boss about my workload. I think I came on too strong. I wish I could take it back."

**Model guides through:** Steps 1, 3, 4 (examine thinking, clarify intent, explore insights)
- "How are you thinking about what happened? What's your model of the conversation?"
- "What did you most want to achieve in that conversation?"
- "What did you learn from the experience that you can use moving forward?"
