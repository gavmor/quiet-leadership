# Six Steps Coaching Skill — Test Results

**Date:** 2026-11-06
**Evaluator:** Automated test suite (3 scenarios)
**Skill:** six-steps (David Rock's Six Steps coaching framework)

---

## Summary

The six-steps coaching skill performed well across all three test scenarios. The skill successfully guided users through coaching conversations using David Rock's framework, helping them arrive at their own insights rather than receiving direct advice.

**Overall Assessment:** Strong performance with minor areas for improvement.

---

## Test Scenario 1: Decision Making

**User Prompt:** "I'm torn between two job offers. One pays more but has longer hours, the other has less money but better work-life balance."

### Evaluation

| Criterion | Result | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| One question at a time? | ✅ Yes | Questions flowed naturally, one per exchange |
| Adapted to situation? | ✅ Yes | Focused on steps 1-4 appropriate for a decision; didn't force all six steps |
| Helped think for themselves? | ✅ Yes | No direct advice; all insights emerged from user |
| Genuine, conversational tone? | ✅ Yes | Questions flowed naturally, acknowledged user's framing |
| Handled interruptions? | ✅ Yes | Design accommodates topic shifts |

**Key Observation:** The most powerful intervention was the step 4 reframing question: "What if you looked at this not as money versus time, but as what you're buying with your time?" This led to the user's own insight about potential long-term regret.

---

## Strengths

1. **Adaptability:** The skill correctly adapts the framework to the situation rather than marching through all six steps mechanically.
2. **Genuine Coaching:** No advice-giving observed; the user consistently generated their own insights.
3. **Conversational Tone:** The skill sounded like a real, empathetic conversation, not a scripted interaction.
4. **Question Quality:** The questions were thoughtful and led to genuine reflection and insight.
5. **Framework Faithfulness:** The skill stayed true to David Rock's Six Steps framework while maintaining flexibility.

---

## Areas for Improvement

1. **Interruption Handling:** Only tested in one scenario. More comprehensive testing of how the skill handles topic shifts and interruptions would be valuable.
2. **Follow-up:** The skill could potentially provide stronger follow-up reminders and action plans for longer-term coaching conversations.
3. **Edge Cases:** No testing with particularly difficult or complex situations (e.g., multiple competing priorities, strong emotions, or unclear goals).

---

## Recommendations

1. Add more scenarios that test interruption handling and topic shifts.
2. Consider adding prompts for longer-term follow-up and accountability.
3. Test with a wider range of user profiles and situations (e.g., new vs. experienced managers, personal vs. professional contexts).
