# Using the Six Steps for Decision Making

> [!tldr] When someone faces a difficult decision, don't tell them what to choose. Use the Six Steps to help them think more clearly about each option, its consequences, and their own preferences.

## Decision Making as Thinking Improvement

The Six Steps are not only useful for solving problems — they're equally powerful when helping someone make a decision. Decisions require careful thinking about:

- The alternatives available
- The likely consequences of each choice
- Personal values and preferences
- Long-term vs. short-term impacts

## Example: Rebecca's Hiring Dilemma

Rebecca, a director of Human Resources at an investment bank, was having trouble deciding between two hires for a senior role. Both candidates had different strengths, and Rebecca was stuck.

Using the Six Steps approach, she was helped to:

1. **Think about thinking** — Recognize that her decision process itself could be improved
2. **Listen for potential** — In what she was saying about both candidates
3. **Speak with intent** — To clarify her own thinking
4. **Dance toward insight** — Through careful questions and reflection
5. **Create new thinking** — Using the CREATE model to explore the decision space
6. **Follow up** — To ensure the decision stuck and was implemented well

## Key Questions for Decision-Making

When someone faces a decision, useful questions include:

- "What are the different options you've considered?"
- "What would be the consequence of each choice?"
- "What do you want this decision to achieve?"
- "How would you know if the decision turned out well?"
- "What do your instincts tell you?"
- "If you had to choose right now, what would you pick?"
- "What's the worst that could happen with each option?"

## The CREATE Model for Decisions

When a decision requires deeper exploration:

1. **C**urrent reality — What's the situation now?
2. **R**esults wanted — What do you want the decision to achieve?
3. **E**xplored options — What are all the alternatives?
4. **A**ction plan — What will you do? (Choose an option)
5. **T**eaching and learning — What will you learn from this decision?

## When to Use This Skill

- An employee faces a career decision
- A manager must choose between strategic options
- Someone is paralyzed by a choice
- A team needs to reach a group decision
- Any situation where careful thinking about alternatives is valuable
