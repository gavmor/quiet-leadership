# Permission and Placement

Two conversation elements essential for [[Dance Toward Insight|creating the environment where people have insights for themselves]].

## Permission

Permission involves creating psychological safety so people feel safe to think deeply and take risks with their thinking. Without permission, people will not be vulnerable enough to explore new ideas or admit what they don't know.

**Creating permission involves:**
- Establishing confidentiality ("What we discuss stays in this room")
- Setting clear expectations about the conversation
- Creating a judgment-free zone
- Acknowledging that different perspectives are valuable
- Making it clear it's safe to be wrong, to try, to explore

**Example language:**
- "You can say anything here; it won't be held against you."
- "There's no right or wrong answer to this."
- "I want to hear your honest thoughts, even if they're not polished."
- "This is a safe space to think out loud."

## Placement

Placement involves clarifying the context of the conversation so people understand what's happening and why. This reduces anxiety and confusion, allowing people to focus on thinking rather than worrying about the social situation.

**Creating placement involves:**
- Explaining the purpose of the conversation
- Setting time boundaries
- Describing your role and their role
- Making clear what kind of thinking is expected
- Providing any relevant background

**Example language:**
- "I'd like to spend 20 minutes exploring your thoughts on X."
- "My role here is to help you think this through, not to tell you what to do."
- "This is a brainstorming session, so any idea is welcome."
- "We're going to focus on Y today, and I'll summarize at the end."

## Why They Matter Together

Permission and placement work together to create what David Rock calls "an environment where people can have insights for themselves." Without permission, people are too anxious to think creatively. Without placement, people are too confused to think effectively.

Both elements address the "dilemma" that often prevents insights — the fear of being wrong or the lack of clarity about what's expected.
