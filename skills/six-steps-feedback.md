# Using the Six Steps for Feedback

> [!tldr] Effective feedback focuses on improving someone's thinking, not critiquing their performance. Give people a chance to think through the feedback and find their own solutions rather than telling them exactly what to do differently.

## The Nature of Good Feedback

Feedback is the delicate art of letting people know the score. It provides information that helps people learn and grow. Feedback is central to good leadership — it's something leaders can do hourly, daily, weekly, or annually.

However, giving feedback is also one of the more difficult conversations leaders must have, with the most potential for things to go wrong. A 2003 Australian business school survey found "coaching staff" in the top five management challenges.

## Traditional vs. Six Steps Feedback

**Traditional feedback approach:**
- Critique what someone did wrong
- Tell them what to do differently
- Focus on performance deficits

**Six Steps feedback approach:**
- Focus on improving thinking
- Give people a chance to reflect and think through the feedback
- Help them generate their own insights
- Create motivation for change from within

## The Feedback Sandwich Is Dead

Forget the old "praise-critique-praise" sandwich. It's formulaic and doesn't actually help people improve their thinking. Instead, use genuine feedback that addresses what's really going on.

## Using the Six Steps for Feedback

### Step 1: Think About Thinking
Consider your own thinking about giving this feedback. Are you approaching it to help or to criticize? What do you actually want to achieve?

### Step 2: Listen for Potential
Before you even speak, listen. What is the person saying? What are they thinking? Listen not just for what went wrong but for what potential exists.

### Step 3: Speak with Intent
Be succinct, specific, and generous. Don't overwhelm them. Choose your words carefully.

### Step 4: Dance Toward Insight
This is the most crucial step. Rather than telling them what to do, help them find their own insights. Use questions like:
- "What did you notice about how that went?"
- "If you were doing it again, what might you do differently?"
- "What do you think worked well? What didn't?"

### Step 5: Create New Thinking
If the feedback involves a complex issue, use the [[create-model]] to help them generate new approaches.

### Step 6: Follow Up
Check in later to see how things are going. Use the [[feeling-model]] to ensure the feedback has led to lasting change.

## Example: Feedback on a Presentation

Instead of saying:
"You spoke too fast and your slides were cluttered."

Try:
"What did you think about how that presentation went? What did you notice about your own delivery and your audience's reaction?"

Wait for them to respond, then gently add your observations as a conversation, not a critique.

## When to Use This Skill

- After someone makes a mistake
- After a presentation or performance
- When you notice a recurring pattern in someone's work
- During regular check-ins
- When someone asks for feedback
- Any time you want to help someone improve
