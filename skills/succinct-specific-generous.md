# Succinct, Specific, and Generous

The three principles of [[Speak with Intent|Speak with Intent]] (Step 3 of transforming performance).

## Be Succinct

Speaks with as few words as possible while still being clear. Succinctness keeps the listener's attention and engagement.

**Key points:**
- We speak at 100 words per minute but think at 600 words per minute
- Succinct speakers get clearer about their own message
- Provides listeners with bite-sized pieces of information rather than overwhelming them
- Gives the listener time to create their own mental maps and make connections
- Avoids "waffling" — speaking in long, uncertain, meandering ways that signal lack of conviction

**Exercise:** Practice explaining yourself in single sentences for one day, or focus on halving the number of words you use.

## Be Specific

Speaks in a way that provides enough detail for the listener to create a clear, unambiguous mental picture of the idea.

**Key points:**
- Listeners need just enough information to understand the exact point
- Avoids conversations going off on unnecessary sidetracks
- Builds trust between speaker and listener
- A definite, observable event occurs when specific: listeners nod at the same moment when they get the point

**Example:**
- Not specific: "That was a great meeting."
- Specific: "That was a great meeting—especially the way we were able to bounce ideas off each other, and how easy it was to get through such a big agenda in two hours."

**Exercise:** Practice being specific for a day, providing the full story in every conversation, but still in summary form.

## Be Generous

Uses words that will have the biggest positive impact on the listener; shows care and commitment to them getting the message.

**Key points:**
- Slowing down to choose words carefully
- Being sensitive to how you come across (not jarring or off-putting)
- Paying attention (giving full focus, not multitasking)
- Acknowledging people with encouragement and validation
- Being personal and real (sharing humanity)
- Creates an environment where people feel safe to think big thoughts

**Example:** "I appreciate you being open about your challenges. I know it might have been a bit of a risk and I appreciate you letting me have a chance to help you here."

**Exercise:** Reread this chapter every day to remind yourself of the principles. Find opportunities to be more aware of how you speak.

## All Three Together

When combined, succinct, specific, and generous communication creates conversations that are:
- Focused and attention-grabbing
- Clear and unambiguous
- Positive and engaging

This creates the conditions for people to have their own insights, which is essential for transforming performance.
