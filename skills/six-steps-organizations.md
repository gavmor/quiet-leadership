# Applying the Six Steps to Whole Organizations

> [!tldr] Organizations can change, but they change from the inside out, not through top-down mandates. The Six Steps principles can be applied at every level to transform organizational culture over time.

## Can Organizations Change?

Yes — but like an individual's brain, an organization can only be changed from the inside out. It won't change because someone tells it to; it needs to have a genuine reason to change.

As Marian Wright Edelman said: "If you don't like the way the world is, you change it. You have an obligation to change it. Just do it one step at a time."

## Key Principles for Organizational Change

### 1. Wide-scale involvement
Change cannot be forced from the top. People at every level need to be involved in creating new patterns of thinking and communication.

### 2. New ways of communicating
When people are asked to think for themselves, they need to have a way to express their thoughts. This means developing new communication norms throughout the organization.

### 3. Time for change
Organizational change takes years, not months or weeks. Just as neural pathways take time to form, organizational culture takes time to transform.

### 4. Leadership role
Leaders play a crucial role in modeling new ways of thinking and communicating. They need to demonstrate the principles themselves before expecting others to adopt them.

## How It Works in Practice

At one large organization, a woman showed David Rock around and expressed her uncertainty about whether the culture could actually change. She didn't believe people could be taught to think differently.

The key is that change happens when people genuinely want it, not when it's forced on them. Leaders need to:
- Create the desire for change
- Provide the tools and skills
- Model the desired behaviors
- Give people time to develop new habits

## The Six Steps at the Organizational Level

### Step 1: Think About Thinking (as an organization)
Help the entire organization reflect on how it thinks and makes decisions. "What's our current thinking about this challenge? Is it working?"

### Step 2: Listen for Potential (across the organization)
Listen to what's happening throughout the organization — not just at the top. What potential do you hear in the discussions at all levels?

### Step 3: Speak with Intent (to the organization)
Communicate clearly with the organization as a whole. Be succinct, specific, and generous in your messaging.

### Step 4: Dance Toward Insight (as an organization)
Use questions that help the entire organization think more deeply. "What if we approached this differently?" "How might we see this from another perspective?"

### Step 5: Create New Thinking (organization-wide)
Use the [[create-model]] with the organization to generate new approaches to its challenges.

### Step 6: Follow Up (across the organization)
Check in regularly to see if the organization's new thinking is leading to better performance.

## When to Use This Skill

- A company is struggling with cultural issues
- Leadership wants to foster innovation
- An organization needs to adapt to changing markets
- You want to develop more independent, creative thinkers throughout the company
- You're trying to improve organizational learning
