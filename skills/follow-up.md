# Follow Up for Lasting Change

After creating new thinking with the [[create-model]], following up is essential to ensure the change sticks. Without follow-up, people may revert to old thinking, especially under stress.

## The Problem Without Follow-Up

The brain has a natural bias toward returning to familiar patterns. When someone tries something new, the new neural pathway is fragile — it's like a footpath through a jungle that can quickly be reclaimed by vegetation if not used regularly. The old, well-worn path (familiar thinking) is easier to travel.

## When to Follow Up

Follow up after:
- Someone commits to specific actions based on new insights
- A period of trying new thinking has passed (usually a few days to a week)
- You sense someone is struggling to maintain new approaches
- Regular check-ins to reinforce progress

## Using the FEELING Model

The [[feeling-model]] provides a structured approach to follow-up:

1. **Find out how they're doing** — Listen to their experiences
2. **Encourage action** — Support continued effort
3. **Evaluate progress** — Help assess results
4. **Look for new ideas** — Explore additional applications
5. **Inspire and energize** — Reinforce enthusiasm
6. **Never give up** — Be patient with the process
7. **Guide them back to their own insights** — Foster self-sufficiency

## Practical Example

Joe created a spreadsheet to analyze project importance (new thinking). His leader:
- Checked in after a week to see how it was going
- Noted Joe had created the spreadsheet but hadn't used it for decision-making
- Encouraged Joe to use it during his next planning meeting
- Followed up again after that meeting to see the impact
- Result: Joe now uses the spreadsheet as a regular part of his process

## Why It Works

Each time someone acts on new thinking, the neural pathway strengthens. Follow-up provides:
- **Accountability** — knowing someone will check in
- **Support** — help when obstacles arise
- **Reinforcement** — recognition of progress
- **Continuity** — bridging the gap between insight and habit
