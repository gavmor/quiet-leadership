## The Feedback Imbalance

Research shows the average employee receives:

- **4-5 minutes of positive feedback per year**
- **2,000-5,000 hours of criticism per year**

This massive imbalance creates a climate of negativity. Most organizations, especially in manufacturing, emphasize the "negative" — errors to correct and problems to fix — as the primary driver of improvement. This approach is not only demoralizing; it fails to produce quality improvements or better working conditions.

## The p = P - I Formula

Performance equals potential minus interference. Interference includes the negative feedback loop of errors → criticism → anxiety → more errors. The formula reveals a profound insight: **removing the negative is as powerful as adding the positive.**

When employees work in an atmosphere of constant criticism, their anxiety increases, which interferes with their ability to perform at their potential. Accentuating the positive reduces that interference.

## Forms of Positive Feedback

Positive feedback comes in six forms:

1. **Appreciation** — "I really like the way you handled that situation."
2. **Validation** — "You're right; I didn't know that."
3. **Recognition** — "That's one of the best proposals I've seen."
4. **Affirmation** — "Yes; let's do that."
5. **Confirmation** — "That sounds right to me."
6. **Thanking** — "Thanks; that made my day."

## The New Feedback Model

Instead of the old "error-criticism" model, ask these questions during and after conversations:

1. What did that person do well during our interaction?
2. What did that person discover during our interaction?
3. What will that person gain in the future as a result of our interaction?
4. How did my interaction make that person feel good about himself or herself?

If you can't answer all four questions, try a new interaction with the same individual.

## FeedForward Technique

Marshall Goldsmith suggests focusing on positive, constructive behaviors for the future, not on past mistakes. When meeting with someone, explain you're meeting to give positive FeedForward rather than negative feedback. Then discuss:

- What worked especially well
- Things you'd like them to do more of
- Positive goals for the future

This approach eliminates defensiveness and builds momentum.

## Connection to Step 1

This skill directly supports [[step-1-think-about-thinking]] by expanding your attention beyond problems and errors. When you actively look for the positive, your thinking shifts from a deficit-oriented mode to a strengths-based mode. You begin to see possibilities where you previously saw only flaws. This expanded thinking creates a different climate in your interactions — one where others feel safe, valued, and capable.

## Practice

For one week, before any conversation, pause and ask: "How can I accentuate the positive with this person?" Look for something genuine to appreciate, validate, or thank them for. Notice how the quality of the interaction changes.
