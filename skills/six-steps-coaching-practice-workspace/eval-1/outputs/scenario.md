**Title:** The Bottleneck at Twenty-Nine

**The Person:** Jake Chen, 29, was promoted to engineering team lead six weeks ago. He's smart, technically excellent, and has been a software developer at this 150-person SaaS company for three years. Jake is a perfectionist—he double-checks his own work obsessively and finds bugs others miss. He genuinely cares about shipping quality code and has always prided himself on delivering reliable, well-tested features. He's introverted and tends to process things internally before speaking up.

**The Situation:** Jake now manages four developers, including one person who was more senior than him before the promotion. The team is three weeks into a major product release, and Jake has become the bottleneck. He's reviewing every pull request himself, rewriting code he considers subpar, and personally handling most customer escalations. His team members have started waiting for his feedback before committing anything. One developer even asked him directly if she could ship without a code review "just this once." The release manager has flagged that Jake's team is tracking behind schedule, and Jake himself is working until 11 p.m. most nights, looking increasingly tired and irritable. His manager mentioned this to you informally, noting that Jake seems to be "doing everyone's job instead of managing them."

**The Challenge for You:** Jake doesn't think there's a problem—he thinks he's being a good leader by ensuring quality and protecting the team from mistakes. If you tell him he needs to delegate more, he'll likely rationalize his behavior as "being thorough" or "stepping in when things aren't going well." This requires helping him examine his own thinking about management (Step 1), recognizing that his perfectionism has value but is now creating a different problem (Step 2), and reframing what "good leadership" means in this context (Step 3). You also need to help him see that his current approach is actually undermining team development and morale, not just slowing them down.

**Conversation Starter:** You've caught Jake in the break room at 7:45 a.m. on a Tuesday. He's staring at his laptop screen with a coffee in one hand, editing someone else's pull request. You pull up a chair. "Rough week?" you ask. He looks up, rubs his face, and says: "We're not going to make the release date. There are too many edge cases nobody thought about, and honestly, half the time I catch things that would have broken in production. I just wish the team would slow down and think before they push code."
