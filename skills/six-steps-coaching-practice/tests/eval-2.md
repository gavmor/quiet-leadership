**Title:** The Master Plumber with the Perfect System

**The Person:** Ed Kowalski, 58, is the most experienced field supervisor at your regional plumbing and heating company. Over 20 years, he's developed his own way of scheduling jobs, reading blueprints, and training apprentices. He's proud of it. His crew is efficient, his rework rate is low, and customers rarely complain. But "efficient" for Ed looks different from what operations wants—and Ed can't articulate why his way works, only that it does.

**The Situation:** The operations director has been collecting data from all regional supervisors. Ed's team completes fewer jobs per week than the regional average, but his customer satisfaction scores are higher. The director is planning a "standardization initiative" where all supervisors would use the same scheduling templates, communication protocols, and performance metrics. Ed hears about it at a regional meeting and gets visibly annoyed. Later that week, you notice he's not filling out the new weekly summary form—he's going back to his old clipboard system. The director is frustrated and asks you to talk to Ed.

**The Challenge for You:** Ed isn't incompetent or lazy; he's developed something that works for his context, but he can't explain it or adapt it. If you come in advocating the standardization effort, he'll dig in. If you validate his approach without pushing change, the director will be angry. You need to understand what's actually in Ed's thinking about his system (Step 1), help him see the value in his approach without romanticizing it (Step 2), and potentially reframe what "change" means for him—not abandoning his system but refining and documenting it (Step 3). The tension is real: his way is good but idiosyncratic, and the company wants consistency across regions.

**Conversation Starter:** You call Ed into your office for a routine check-in. He sits down, opens his worn leather notebook, and says: "I figured out why the new form doesn't make sense. I write the job codes in a different order because that's how I think through them. But nobody asks that anymore. I just do it my way." He looks up expectantly, clearly wanting your reaction to this revelation.
