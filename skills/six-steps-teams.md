# Using the Six Steps with Teams

> [!tldr] The Six Steps can be applied to groups, not just individuals. Facilitate team conversations that help the entire group improve its thinking about a problem or challenge.

## Working with Teams

Teams, like individuals, benefit enormously from having their thinking improved. The Six Steps can be adapted to work with groups of two to 20 or more people.

As Mike Krzyzewski (Duke University basketball coach) said: "You develop a team to achieve what one person cannot accomplish alone. All of us alone are weaker, by far, than if all of us are together."

## Team Coaching vs. Individual Coaching

The principles are the same, but the execution differs:

- **Listen to multiple perspectives** — You're listening for potential in what the whole group is saying
- **Create a safe environment** — The team needs to feel safe to share ideas and take risks
- **Facilitate group reflection** — Help the team see the patterns in its own thinking
- **Watch for group dynamics** — Notice who's talking, who's quiet, who dominates, who withdraws

## Example: The News Team

Antoine, a cable TV journalist newly promoted to manage a team of writers and camera operators, struggled with his new role. He had been a writer but now needed to help his team improve their work.

Using the Six Steps approach, he:
1. Listened to what each team member had to say about their work
2. Asked how they thought they could improve
3. Helped them see patterns in their own thinking
4. Encouraged them to come up with their own solutions
5. Followed up to see if changes were happening

## Applying the Six Steps to Teams

### Step 1: Think About Thinking (as a group)
Help the team reflect on how they've been approaching the problem. "What's our current thinking about this? Is it helping us?"

### Step 2: Listen for Potential (in the group)
Listen not just to individual members but to the group as a whole. What potential do you hear in their collective discussion?

### Step 3: Speak with Intent (to the group)
Be clear in your communication to the team. Model the kind of thinking you want to see.

### Step 4: Dance Toward Insight (as a group)
Use questions that help the whole team think more deeply. "What if we approached this differently?" "How might we see this from another perspective?"

### Step 5: Create New Thinking (as a group)
Use the [[create-model]] with the team to generate new approaches. "What else could we try?" "What would success look like?"

### Step 6: Follow Up (with the team)
Check in later to see if the team's new thinking has led to better performance.

## When to Use This Skill

- A team is stuck on a problem
- Team members aren't working well together
- A group needs to make a decision
- You're facilitating a team meeting or workshop
- A team needs to develop a strategy or plan
