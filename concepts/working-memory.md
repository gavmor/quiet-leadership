# Working Memory Limits

Working memory is the brain's limited capacity for conscious, active processing. Understanding this limit is crucial for effective leadership and communication.

## The Seven-Concept Limit

Research by Joseph LeDoux and others suggests the brain can hold just seven concepts in working memory at any one time. Anyone who has tried to juggle too many projects at once has experienced this limitation firsthand.

## Implications

- Complex problem-solving in real-time is difficult because we can't hold all relevant information simultaneously
- This is why [[hard-wiring]] exists — the brain automates what it can to free up working memory for higher-level tasks
- Leaders should avoid overwhelming people with too many ideas at once
- Break complex problems into manageable parts that can fit within working memory
- [[the-iceberg-model]] explains why much of performance happens below conscious awareness

## In Leadership Conversations

- One conversation should focus on one issue or topic
- Avoid multi-topic "kitchen sink" meetings
- Use models like [[create-model]] that break problems into sequential phases
- Let people process one concept at a time rather than bombarding them with information
