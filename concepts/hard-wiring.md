# Hard Wiring

The brain constantly tries to automate processes by pushing frequently-used or emotionally charged maps into long-term storage in the subcortex, creating what we call "hard wiring."

## How It Works

- The brain has limited [[working-memory]] (about seven concepts)
- Actions or thoughts that are repeated or tagged as important (linked to emotional charge) get "hard coded"
- The brain pushes these maps down into the subcortex, which has far more capacity than working memory
- Like water carving a path through rock (e.g., Grand Canyon), repeated neural pathways become deeper and more ingrained

## Characteristics

- Once hard-wired, processes become unconscious — we don't "have in mind" what we're doing
- Well-trodden paths are much easier to follow than cutting new routes
- Hard-wired skills are more dependable than conscious processing
- Elite athletes use less of their brain while performing — their cognitive brain no longer needs to manage their movements

## Implications for Leadership

- Most workplace behaviors are deeply hard-wired and therefore habitual
- Changing habits requires more effort than we typically apply
- When external realities change, people's internal realities (perceptions) often don't change as quickly
- To change behavior, we need to work with the brain's tendency to create new pathways, not just tell people to change
