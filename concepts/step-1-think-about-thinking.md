## Core Idea

The first step to being a Quiet Leader is to think about people's thinking. In other words, to become passionate about improving not what people are thinking about, but the way they think.

## Five Elements

Step 1 contains five elements:

1. **[Let Them Do All the Thinking](../skills/let-them-do-all-the-thinking.md)** — The underlying and most important principle in the whole book. Without this approach, nothing that comes after it will help.

2. **[Focus on Solutions](../skills/focus-on-solutions.md)** — Keep people focused on solutions, not problems, so that conversations are as useful as possible.

3. **[Remember to Stretch](../skills/remember-to-stretch.md)** — Stretch and challenge people, to open up their thinking, not just be supportive.

4. **[Accentuate the Positive](../skills/accentuate-the-positive.md)** — Focus on the positive, on what people are doing well, so that you grow people's strengths.

5. **[Put Process Before Content](../skills/put-process-before-content.md)** — Make it easy for people to think by having a clear process behind every conversation.

## Self-Directed Learning

A key principle is that people need to make their own connections about anything you tell them. You'll never guess the right answer for them anyway. Letting people find their own answers:

- Allows people to be energized by new connections (the "aha" moment)
- Is less effort
- Is faster (takes only 10-20% of the time compared to telling people what to do)
- Makes people more likely to adopt good ideas

As Sir John Whitmore says: "To tell denies or negates another's intelligence. To ask honors it."

## Performance Formula

From W. Timothy Gallwey's work: **p = P - I** (performance equals potential minus interference). Our inner voice of self-criticism gets in the way of our natural abilities. Quiet Leaders help reduce interference by listening for potential and accentuating the positive.

## Key Quote

> One cannot teach a man anything. One can only enable him to learn from within himself.
> — Galileo Galilei (1564–1642)
