## Core Idea

The second step to being a Quiet Leader is to actively listen for people's potential. Listen to people as someone capable, strong, and fully able to come up with great ideas and solutions — not as someone in difficulty who needs help.

## Neuroscience of Listening

Based on neuroscience research, we literally only hear what we listen for. Our neurons are primed to seek out certain bits of information based on the mental frames we hold. Prediction is the primary function of the neocortex, and the foundation of intelligence.

When you listen to people, unless you consciously choose a certain way to listen, you will listen to prove your existing theories about this person. This is hardly the most effective way of transforming performance.

## Listening for Potential

A Quiet Leader listens and believes in others completely. They encourage and support others in being the best they can be, just in how they listen, without saying a word. They listen to people as though they have all the tools they need to be successful, and could simply benefit from exploring their thoughts and ideas out loud.

When you listen for potential, you assume that others have the capacity to answer the question for themselves; you then respectfully see how you can best make yourself useful.

## The Clarity of Distance Model

Listening for potential requires identifying and putting aside mental states that cloud our ability to listen openly. There are four mental frames that can get in the way:

1. **Lost in the details** — We get lost in the tangled forest of information and can't see what's going on. We need to be the guide standing on a platform above the trees, who can see the qualities of the forest itself.

2. **Misled by our filters** — Filters are the unconscious mental frames through which we see, the sum of our assumptions, expectations, predictions, and decisions about anything. When we listen through filters, we are fitting people into our predetermined boxes.

3. **Having an agenda** — We're suddenly too close, we're lost in our own agenda instead of seeing the other person's possibilities.

4. **Hot spots** — Charged issues that we're lost in the emotions of. When you're trying to listen to someone as their potential and the conversation results in either of you tripping over a hot spot, the best course of action could be to head home for the day.

## Examples of Listening for Potential

| When someone says... | Listening for potential response |
|---------------------|----------------------------------|
| "I'm not sure what to do about this project." | "How can I best help you think this through? Do you want to use me as a sounding board?" |
| "I feel like I made a mess of that project." | "Sounds like you're having a rough time. What have you learned from taking on this client so far?" |
