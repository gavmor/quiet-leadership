# Speak with Intent

In step three of [[Quiet Leadership (David Rock)|transforming performance as a Quiet Leader]], we explore what it takes to speak so that you have the maximum possible likelihood of improving people's thinking.

## Intent vs. Impact

Rock identifies a critical concept: **intent versus impact** — the gap between what we intend to communicate and what others actually receive. Drawing on neuroscience, he argues our communication efficiency is roughly 66%, similar to early Internet protocols. Most workplace communication has impact that differs from intent.

## Three Core Patterns

After observing many workplace conversations, Rock identified three core patterns that impede effective communication:

1. **Lack of succinctness**: Speakers take too long, causing listeners to "check out"
2. **Lack of specificity**: Listeners don't understand exactly what is being said
3. **Lack of generosity**: Speakers don't use language the listener can grasp

These three patterns led to the three-part model: **be succinct, be specific, be generous.**

## Communication-Saturated World

We live in a world flooded with information. Rock argues that the quality of day-to-day conversations in many workplaces is "excruciatingly low." While poor conversational skills may get by day-to-day, transforming performance requires a different level — like the difference between casually riding a bicycle versus hurtling down a freeway at 50 mph.
