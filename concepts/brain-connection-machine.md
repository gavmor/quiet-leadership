## Core Concept

The human brain operates fundamentally as a **connection machine**. Its underlying functionality is finding associations, connections, and links between bits of information. What we experience as thoughts, memories, skills, and attributes are vast sets of neural connections that David Rock calls "maps."

## Key Principles

- **Map complexity**: Our mental maps are extraordinarily rich, containing links between cognitive centers, motor skills, sensory data, and emotional responses. A single concept like "car" connects to memories, sounds, histories, and practical knowledge.
- **Constant connection-making**: The brain makes over a million new connections every second, continuously building and updating our neural landscape.
- **Order from chaos**: The brain seeks to make links between information so that life makes sense—we feel better surrounded by order and symmetry.
- **Predictive function**: Mental maps help us predict outcomes more easily, which Jeff Hawkins argues is our most differentiating attribute from other animals.

## The "Aha" Moment

When we process new ideas, we create a mental map and compare it to existing maps. If connections form, we experience that sudden "aha" moment—the creation of a new map. This:
- Releases energy and motivation
- Changes brain waves and neurotransmitter activity
- Produces a strong drive to act

## Implications for Leadership

- **Committed action requires personal thinking**: People must create their own mental maps to take committed action
- **Inertia exists**: People experience inertia around thinking for themselves due to the energy required
- **Create space for connections**: Leaders should focus on helping people make their own connections rather than thinking for them
